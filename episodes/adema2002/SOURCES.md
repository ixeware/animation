# "149–0" (AS Adema 149–0 SO l'Emyrne, Madagascar, 2002) — sources

AS Adema 149–0 Stade Olympique de l'Emyrne (SOE): Madagascar's national championship (the THB Champions League), the
last match of its four-team final stage, at Toamasina, on Thursday 31 October 2002. SOE, the defending champions, scored
all 149 goals into their own net in protest at the refereeing: in their previous match, against DSA Antananarivo, a late
and disputed penalty given against them ended their title hopes, and AS Adema clinched the title with a game to spare.
The federation (FMF) banned SOE's coach, Ratsimandresy Ratsarazaka, for three years and suspended several players.

## Left out on purpose, or not known

- **The score of the previous match** (2–2) is in one source only (Wikipedia): the film says "a late, disputed penalty"
  and shows no score.
- **How many players were suspended** differs (four in Wikipedia, three in These Football Times, which says the national
  team's captain only got a warning): the film says "several" and names nobody but the coach.
- **The referee's name** (These Football Times only) is not given.
- **The annulment of SOE's 2002 results and the club's end in 2006** (Wikipedia, the second marked as needing a source):
  not in the film.
- **"The craziest scoreline in football"** is the brief's word, an opinion, not a record. The record the film states is
  Guinness's: the most own goals in a domestic league match.

## What is drawn (cartoons, not likenesses)

- The kits follow the clubs' colours in their Wikipedia infoboxes today: SO l'Emyrne in yellow, AS Adema in blue. The
  kits worn that day were not checked.
- The ground is drawn from the general look of a stadium in a tropical port town, not the Barikadimy Stadium itself. The
  time of day is not known: it is drawn in afternoon light.
- The scoreboard that climbs to 149 is the film's own picture (whether the ground had a scoreboard is not known).
- The coach's tactics board, with its arrow into their own net, is a picture of "the plan", not a scene from a source.
- The fans are cartoons. Their confused faces are the brief's ("confused fans"); the sources say they asked for refunds.
- Not checked, drawn: DSA Antananarivo in white; the goalkeepers in green (SOE) and orange (AS Adema); the referee in
  black; the ticket booth in the outer wall with its "TICKETS" sign; the dressing room. The previous match is drawn at
  the same ground, since the whole final stage was played at Toamasina (RSSSF).
- The kick-off shown is SO l'Emyrne's, with 1–0 already on the board: AS Adema kicked off the match (Wikipedia), and
  after every goal the side that conceded it kicks off (Law 8), so every later restart was SOE's. In 2002 the ball had
  to go forward from the kick-off, so the drawn tap goes forward before the man who takes it turns back.
- "36 s" on the stopwatch is 90 minutes divided by 149 goals (36.2 s); the hand moves 36 s of a 60-second dial per goal.
  The board holds 50 and 100 while they are said, then races on: a picture of the count, not the real timing of the goals.
- The 2001 team photograph, the gold cup and its ribbons turning from yellow to blue are pictures of "the defending
  champions" and "handed the title", not photographs or the real trophy.
- The coach (with a beard), the players and the referee are cartoons, not likenesses; no face is drawn after a photograph.
