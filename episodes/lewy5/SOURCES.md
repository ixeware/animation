# Five goals in nine minutes — sources

FC Bayern München 5–1 VfL Wolfsburg, Bundesliga 2015–16, matchday 6, Allianz Arena, Tuesday 22 September 2015
(an evening kick-off under floodlights).

The brief was the user's idea no. 2, "5 Goals in 9 mins": show the key moments, all five goals and Pep Guardiola's
reactions. Everything below was checked against match reports and against the Bundesliga's own uncut video of the nine
minutes before it was drawn.

## Details taken from the footage (not from text sources)

- Bayern played in all red; Wolfsburg in white shirts with a green band across the chest, green shorts and white
  socks; Diego Benaglio in dark blue; Manuel Neuer in light blue with dark shorts.
- Bayern's players wore a black armband (Lewandowski on his right arm).
- In the second half Bayern attacked one goal throughout; seen from the attackers, the second goal went low to the
  left, the third came back off the left post, the cross for the fourth came from the byline on the right, and the
  fifth went into the top right corner from Götze's cross from the right.
- After the fifth goal Lewandowski ran away with his right arm raised; team-mates caught him up (Douglas Costa first).
- Guardiola wore a dark suit over a dark jumper; he is bald with a short grey beard. After the fifth goal the bench
  behind him were embracing.
- The stopwatch on screen reads the official times at the third, fourth and fifth goals. Its reading at the second
  goal (about 1:01) is my own measurement from the uncut video, not an official figure.

## What is drawn, not documented

- The figures are cartoons, not likenesses. Shirt numbers are the players' real ones (Lewandowski 9, Müller 25, Costa
  11, Götze 19, Boateng 17, Alaba 27, Neuer 1; Benaglio 1, Dante 18, Naldo 25, Rodríguez 34, Luiz Gustavo 22,
  Draxler 10, Caligiuri 7). Every named player who appears was in the starting line-up or, in Lewandowski's case, on
  the bench (Sports Mole team news). The substitutes and staff around the dugout are not individuals.
- Where exactly each defender stood, and the exact flight of each ball, are drawn after the footage but are not
  measured. Which Wolfsburg player is nearest in each attack follows the line-up, not an identification from the
  footage: Ricardo Rodríguez was the left-back, so he is the defender on Bayern's right flank (the first, fourth and
  fifth goals); Luiz Gustavo, a holding midfielder, is the one outside the box at the second. For Caligiuri's goal only
  the side he shot from is sourced; the corner it went into is not checked.
- The goalkeepers' dives are drawn: that Benaglio was beaten each time is the fact, how he went down is not taken from
  the footage.
- Guardiola with his hands on his hips at half-time, and the hand on Lewandowski's arm as he sends him on, are
  staging. What is taken from the footage: the instructions to Boateng (after the first and the fourth goal) and the
  hands on the head after the fifth.
- "I can't explain it" is shown at an interview wall; the quote is real, the wall and the gesture are staging.
- The four records are listed over a shot of him on the pitch with the match ball; the certificates were in fact
  handed over on 30 November 2015 in Munich.
- The last shot, him on the bench in the first half: that he started as a substitute is the fact; what he wore and
  who sat near him are drawn.
- The advertising boards carry the date and the fixture instead of the real sponsors.

## Left out

- A sixth goal was close: in the 69th minute Ricardo Rodríguez cleared Lewandowski's effort off the line.
- The previous fastest Bundesliga hat-trick was Michael Tönnies's for MSV Duisburg in 1991 (Guinness gives six
  minutes, other sources five), so the film only says how long Lewandowski's took.
- Wolfsburg had come to Munich unbeaten in the league that season, as cup holders and the previous season's
  runners-up.
