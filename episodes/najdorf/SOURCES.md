| Warsaw, the rising star of Polish chess | born 1910 (Grodzisk Mazowiecki), Warsaw champion 1934, pupil of Tartakower, four pre-war Olympiads for Poland | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Najdorf) |
| In one game he gives up both bishops and both knights and mates with a pawn | "Polish Immortal", Glücksberg–Najdorf, Warsaw: 9...Bxh2+ 13...Bg1 16...Ndxe5+ 17...Nxe5+ 20...Bg4+ 21...Ne5+ 22...h5#. Date disputed (Kasparov: 1928; others 1930 or 1935), so no year is shown | [Wikipedia: Polish Immortal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Immortal) |
| 1939: he sails to Buenos Aires for the Chess Olympiad | 8th Olympiad, Teatro Politeama, Buenos Aires, 21 Aug – 19 Sep 1939; Najdorf board 2 for Poland (+12 −2 =4), Poland second | [Wikipedia: 8th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Chess_Olympiad), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Najdorf) |
| At home, his wife Genia and their daughter Lusia | wife Genia, daughter Lusia (no ages or likenesses given; shown only as silhouettes) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Najdorf) |
| September 1: Germany invades Poland | the Olympiad finals began on 1 September 1939, the day the war broke out | [Wikipedia: 8th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Chess_Olympiad) |
| He stays in Argentina; no news | "after the Olympiad Najdorf decided to stay in Argentina"; "he had no news of his family" | [ChessBase](https://en.chessbase.com/post/remembering-miguel-najdorf), [ChessBase](https://en.chessbase.com/newsroom/post/48-blindfold-boards-the-tale-behind-the-record) |
| 1943: forty boards blindfolded, a world record; no answer | 40 boards in 1943, a record, "with no happy end" | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Najdorf), [ChessBase](https://en.chessbase.com/newsroom/post/48-blindfold-boards-the-tale-behind-the-record) |
| 1947: twenty-three hours, thirty-nine wins | over 23 hours (Guinness: 23 h 25 min), 39 wins, 4 draws, 2 losses | [Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/455342-most-simultaneous-blindfolded-chess-wins-by-win-percentage) |
| Newspapers around the world print his name; no one ever wrote back | "covered by the press all over the world it did not bring him news of his family"; "But he did not hear from his family" | [ChessBase](https://en.chessbase.com/post/remembering-miguel-najdorf), [Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/455342-most-simultaneous-blindfolded-chess-wins-by-win-percentage) |
| His wife, daughter, parents and four siblings were murdered in the Holocaust | "His wife Genia, daughter Lusia, parents Gdalik and Raisa, and four siblings were murdered in the Holocaust." (ChessBase: he knew of his wife's and child's deaths before 1945; the video does not say when he learned) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Najdorf), [ChessBase](https://en.chessbase.com/post/remembering-miguel-najdorf) |
| He stays, becomes Miguel, starts again | Argentine citizen 1944, publicly known as Miguel; remarried (Adela, 1947); worked in insurance; died 4 July 1997 in Málaga, aged 87 | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Najdorf) |
| The line he made famous, 5...a6, the Najdorf; the weapon of Fischer and Kasparov | 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 a6; the idea is credited to Opočenský, "it was Najdorf who made the variation popular" (hence "made famous", not "invented"); "two world champions who formed much of their careers using the Najdorf system" | [ChessBase](https://en.chessbase.com/post/remembering-miguel-najdorf), [Wikipedia: Najdorf Variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Defence,_Najdorf_Variation) |
| Still one of the most popular openings | "one of the most popular openings in modern chess" | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Najdorf) |
