# North Korea 0–7 Portugal (World Cup 2010) — sources

Checked on 10 November 2026. The idea came as "the team that played Squid Game on a football field: publicly shamed
for six hours, rumours that they were executed, nobody knows what happened to some of them". Checked against the
reports, the story is narrower than that, and the video says only what the sources support:

- the match and the live broadcast are documented;
- the six-hour criticism session is one report (Radio Free Asia, anonymous sources), never confirmed, denied by North
  Korea's football association;
- the mines and the executions were rumours. All eleven men who started the match were in North Korea's squad six
  months later, so for them the rumours were false;
- the one person whose fate is not known is the coach.

"Squid Game" in the opening line is a figure of speech (a game with a price for losing). Nothing from the series is
shown: no costumes, masks, shapes, sets or music.

Also reported, not in the video: the television pictures reached Pyongyang free through the Asia-Pacific Broadcasting
Union after South Korea's rights holder declined to supply them; one outlet reported that the commentary stopped after
the fourth goal (not used: AP quotes the commentator at the final whistle); North Korea lost its third match 3–0 to
Ivory Coast; years later An Yong-hak, who played in the match, dismissed the stories about the team
([AP via Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/world/north-korea-watches-live-as-its-team-loses-big),
[CNN, 2024](https://www.cnn.com/2024/06/11/sport/north-korea-soccer-team-inside-spt-intl/index.html)).

Left out on purpose, and why:
- any claim that players were executed or sent to camps: nothing supports it, and the squad list six months later
  contradicts it. The video names these as rumours and then shows what can be checked;
- what was said in the hall, and what happened to the coach afterwards (expelled from the party, sent to a building
  site): reported as rumour by the same source, "hard to verify"; the video stops at "never been confirmed";
- the reported charge against the coach (that he had "betrayed" Kim Jong-un): one anonymous report; not needed;
- no leader is shown or named beyond the AP's wording of the broadcast ("praising the leader").

On screen, drawn rather than documented:
- every person is a cartoon, not a likeness; the faces are generic;
- kits: North Korea in their white change kit against Portugal and in red in the other scenes; Portugal all in red
  (a kit site's notes on this match say Portugal wore red shorts because North Korea's change kit had white ones);
  the goalkeeper's green is a guess; none of it is the exact 2010 design
  ([True Colours](https://www.truecoloursfootballkits.com/?p=2172));
- the seven balls in the net in the opening are a figure for the seven goals; a match has one ball;
- the six second-half goals are shown as six quick cuts of a ball going in, with the scorer and the minute; only the
  first goal of the match follows the description of how it was scored. Where each player stood is not documented;
- the anthem line-up stands for their first match of the tournament (at night, in red); who stood where is drawn;
- the goal against Brazil is drawn as a run onto a pass and a shot past the goalkeeper: who scored and when are from the
  reports, how he scored it is not (no description was found). Brazil in yellow and blue, their goalkeeper in grey;
- the living room, the television set and the three people watching are drawn: a home in Pyongyang, not a real one.
  The picture on the set is drawn; the scoreline card for the Brazil match and the factory clip are illustrations of
  what AP describes, not the real broadcast. The label "Korean Central Television" follows AP's "Korean Central
  Broadcasting commentator";
- the hall, the banners, the lectern, the two rows of players in tracksuits, the commentator pointing, the minister
  beside him and the audience are drawn from Radio Free Asia's description. Nobody outside has shown the room. The
  light that goes from one player to the next is a picture of the commentator naming each one's shortcomings; the
  players pointing at their coach is a picture of "required to criticize" him, not a reported gesture. The two
  exempted players are not singled out in the picture;
- the clock that runs from ten to four stands for six hours; the real times are not reported;
- the figures going dark in the fog are a picture of the rumours, not of anything that happened;
- the two letters are illustrations: FIFA's question and the federation's answer are quoted from the reports, the
  sheets themselves are drawn;
- the squad line-up in daylight stands for the Asian Cup squad of January 2011 (the counter runs to 11 of 11);
- the coach on an empty touchline, fading, is a picture of "nothing has been confirmed", not of his fate.
