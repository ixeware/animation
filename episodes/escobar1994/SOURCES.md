# "The gentleman of football" (Andrés Escobar, 1994) — sources

USA 2–1 Colombia, FIFA World Cup, Group A, the Rose Bowl, Pasadena, California, Wednesday 22 June 1994. Andrés Escobar
(Colombia, No. 2) scored an own goal at 35' (11v11 has 34'), stretching to cut out a cross from John Harkes; Earnie
Stewart made it 2–0 (52'), Adolfo Valencia scored for Colombia in the 90th minute. Colombia, beaten 3–1 by Romania four
days earlier, were out of the World Cup on 26 June, last in the group. Escobar was shot dead in the car park of the El
Indio nightclub in Medellín in the early hours of 2 July 1994, ten days after the match. He was 27.

The narration, in full: "Andrés Escobar. Colombia's number two. One touch at the World Cup, and ten days later, he was
gone. / In 1994, Colombia came to the World Cup as one of the favourites. Months earlier, they had crushed Argentina 5–0
in Buenos Aires. / But they lost their first game. Then, the hosts: the USA. A cross came in. Escobar stretched, and
turned it into his own net. Colombia lost 2–1. Four days later, they were out. / He wrote in a newspaper: see you soon,
because life doesn't end here. / On July 2, ten days after that match, he was shot dead outside a nightclub in Medellín.
He was 27. / More than 100,000 people came to his funeral. Today his statue stands in Medellín, and they still call him
the gentleman of football."

## Left out on purpose, or not known

- **The shooting is not shown** (the brief). The night is an empty car park under street lights, the date and the years;
  no figure, no gun, no sound of shots.
- **Why he was killed is disputed** (an argument in the car park; gamblers' losses; the cartels). The film does not say
  why. "One touch at the World Cup, and ten days later, he was gone" puts the two dates side by side and no more.
- **The shout of "goal" at the shooting** is in one report only: not used.
- **The killer** (Humberto Muñoz Castro, sentenced to 43 years, freed in 2005): not in the film.
- **The date of the El Tiempo column** differs between sources (24 or 29 June 1994): the film gives none.
- **The year of the statue** differs (2001 or 2002): the film gives none.
- **The minute of the own goal**: 35' (Wikipedia, DFB) or 34' (11v11): the film shows none.

## What is drawn (cartoons, not likenesses)

- Escobar, his teammates, the USA players, Harkes and Córdoba are drawn figures. No sponsor, no real badge.
- **Kits.** The photograph below shows Escobar after the own goal in a yellow shirt and blue Umbro shorts: Colombia are
  drawn so. Their socks (drawn red), Córdoba's kit (grey) and the USA's (white) are not in it and were not confirmed by
  any source that could be read (kit archive pages were unreachable).
- Argentina 0–5 Colombia is drawn at night: Trivela calls it Colombia's "noite mágica" at the Monumental and Spanish-language
  coverage speaks of "la noche del 5 de septiembre" (the kick-off time itself was not found). Only the Argentine goalkeeper
  is in the picture; the goals come from far off, so no Colombian kit of that night is drawn.
  https://trivela.com.br/america-do-sul/ha-25-anos-a-colombia-vivia-uma-noite-magica-5x0-sobre-a-argentina/
- The own goal is drawn from the facts above (Harkes's cross from the US left, Escobar stretching, the ball past Córdoba);
  where each man stood and the camera angle are ours, slowed down as a replay.
- The Rose Bowl is the drawn Rose Bowl of `baggio1994`, with a crowd in Colombian yellow, blue and red and American
  white, red and blue.
- The newspaper is a plain drawn page with no masthead, the quote in Spanish with the English under it.
- Medellín at night is drawn from its general look (a city filling a valley, lights up the slopes), not one view.
- The statue is drawn as a bronze footballer with a ball on a pedestal; the real one's pose is not copied.
