# Chess Records That Sound Fake — facts and sources

The hook teases #1 ("dozens of games at once, blindfolded — how many?") and the answer, 48, comes at the end.

- #5 Ivan Nikolić vs Goran Arsović, Belgrade 1989: 269 moves, about 20 hours 15 minutes, drawn. Played under the special rule of the time that allowed 100 moves in certain endgames (ChessBase, "A Game That Outlasted the Day"; Lichess blog "List of world records in chess"). The endgame on screen is illustrative, not the actual game. The two players are generic cartoons.
- #4 Magnus Carlsen: 125 classical games without a loss, 31 July 2018 – 10 October 2020 (42 wins, 83 draws), ended by Jan-Krzysztof Duda at Norway Chess 2020 (Chess.com; ChessBase; The Spectator). The order of wins and draws along the row of boards is illustrative.
- #3 Bobby Fischer: 20 consecutive wins in 1970–71, the last seven games of the Palma de Mallorca Interzonal, 6–0 against Mark Taimanov (Candidates quarterfinal), 6–0 against Bent Larsen (Candidates semifinal) and the first game against Tigran Petrosian. Some historians count 19 because the Panno game was a forfeit (Chess.com; ChessBase).
- #2 Longest chess marathon: 64 hours, Tunde Onakoya and Shawn Martinez, Times Square, New York, 17–20 April 2025, 473 games (Guinness World Records; Chess.com).
- #1 Timur Gareyev: 48 simultaneous blindfold games, Las Vegas, 3–4 December 2016, +35 =7 −6, riding an exercise bike; recognized by Guinness (Chess.com; ChessBase). The ring of tables in the arena is a stylized staging, not the real room layout.
