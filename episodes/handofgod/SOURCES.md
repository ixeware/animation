On screen, drawn rather than documented (the facts behind each are in the table above):
- every person is a cartoon, not a likeness; small on the pitch, they are told apart by hair, build, kit and number;
- the positions of the players in both goals are a reconstruction from the descriptions above, not tracking data:
  the order of the defenders and the outcome are right, the exact paths and distances are drawn. The run is played at
  about real pace (about 8 m/s); the ball meets the fist in bullet time for the narration;
- the Hand of God's ball path (from Hodge, over Shilton) and the camera's ring round the fist are illustration;
- the crowd, the advertising boards (place names and the date in Spanish, no sponsors) and the stadium are drawn after
  the Azteca's general shape, not to plan;
- the referee pointing to the centre circle, the England players' protests and Maradona's celebrations are the usual
  gestures, not frame-accurate;
- the press room, the backdrop, the microphones and the photographers are our cartoon; the gestures (finger to the
  temple, a finger to the sky) illustrate the quote;
- the radio booth with its window on the pitch is drawn; the kite with a 10 in the sky is a visual pun on "barrilete";
- Butcher's challenge is drawn as one slide at Maradona's feet; his last lunge as Maradona shot is left out (he is
  drawn trailing behind), so that no one stands between the camera and Maradona at the finish;
- the trophy is a generic gold cup, not the real World Cup trophy's design; the lift (on his feet, among four
  teammates) is staged, not a copy of any photograph;
- the shirt swap: Maradona walking off with Hodge's white shirt in his hand and Hodge holding up the 10 are drawn;
  only the swap itself and the place (by the dressing rooms) are from Hodge's account;
- the shop is drawn as a street market (Flashscore names the Tepito district); the man comparing two blue shirts is the
  kit man's search (Wikipedia: Bilardo could not choose between two; Maradona picked one) — his face is generic;
- the auction: the gallery, the glass case, the screen with the bids and the "SOLD" stamp illustrate an online sale
  that closed on 4 May 2022; no auction house is named or imitated on screen;
- the CTA's five defenders stand in for the five England players he went past.

Not in the video, on purpose: Shilton's and other England players' bitterness, the 2020 death of Maradona, the later
sale of the match ball by the referee (its sales and prices are reported inconsistently).
