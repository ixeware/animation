# "It's a disgrace" — sources

Chelsea 1–1 FC Barcelona (1–1 on aggregate, Barcelona through on away goals), UEFA Champions League semi-final,
second leg, Stamford Bridge, London, Wednesday 6 May 2009. Referee: Tom Henning Øvrebø (Norway). Attendance 37,857.
The first leg at the Camp Nou on 28 April had ended 0–0.

The brief was the user's idea no. 4, "ITS A DISGRACE": the key moments, the penalty appeals Chelsea were denied,
Iniesta's late equaliser and Didier Drogba's outburst at the camera. Everything below was checked against match
reports and against television footage of the match before it was drawn.

## Details taken from the footage (not from text sources)

- Chelsea in blue shirts and shorts with white socks; Barcelona in their yellow away shirts with a thin blue-and-red
  stripe down the middle, dark blue shorts and yellow socks. Petr Čech in orange with black shorts and his black head
  guard; Víctor Valdés in black. Øvrebø, shaven-headed, in a dark grey shirt with red piping.
- Chelsea attacked the goal to the left of the main camera in the first half (Essien's goal) and the other one in the
  second; Iniesta scored into the goal on the left, into the top corner to Čech's left.
- Iniesta ran off with his arms out and pulled his shirt off; Guardiola, in a dark suit, ran down the touchline.
- After the last appeal Ballack ran beside the referee, shouting at his shoulder. At the final whistle Drogba, in his
  match shirt, was held back by stewards in orange vests near the tunnel.

## What is drawn, not documented

- The cast are cartoons, not likenesses. The kits are drawn from the footage without sponsors' names or club badges
  (Chelsea's shorts a shade deeper than the shirt so that the two read apart in flat colour; the real ones were the
  same blue);
  the boards round the pitch carry the date and the fixture instead of advertising; the mark on the television
  picture is a plain "LIVE", not a broadcaster's.
- The order: the film opens on the end of the story (Drogba at the camera, seen through its lens), then the tape
  "runs back" with a clock counting down from 95 to 0. That is a device; the same picture returns in its place near
  the end.
- Where each man stands, how he runs and who is nearest the ball are staged. The footage fixed the place of each
  incident, the end of the ground and who was involved, not every position. In the build-up to Iniesta's goal only
  Messi, Iniesta, Essien, Terry, Alex, Eto'o and Čech are drawn; at the kick-off the teams stand in their formations.
- The first appeal is seen along the side line of the penalty area, with labels "THE BOX" and "OUTSIDE" lying on the
  grass and a ring round the spot of the free kick: graphics. The contact is drawn on the line, the spot just outside
  it, which is how it was given. The rings round Abidal's hand, Piqué's hand and Eto'o's arm are graphics too; the
  hand of Piqué and the shot on Eto'o's arm are shown slowed down.
- How the men fall is drawn: Malouda pitches forward, slides on his front and comes up on one elbow to appeal;
  Drogba and Anelka go down forward; Drogba appeals on his knees.
- After the hand of Piqué, Anelka turns to the referee, who has run up to within two metres of him: staged that close
  so that both faces are in one picture (in the footage the referee is further off).
- The referee's signals (the whistle at his mouth, both arms swept apart for "play on", the pointing to the spot, the
  card, the other arm showing the way off) are a referee's ordinary signals, drawn; so are Abidal's hands on his head.
- Guardiola's run down the touchline is from the footage; the three men behind him are drawn.
- Ballack running at the referee's shoulder is from the footage; how far and with what gestures is drawn, and his
  booking is not shown.
- Full time: Terry, Lampard sat on the grass with his hands on it, and the three Barcelona players jumping are drawn.
- Drogba leaving the bench: Hiddink going a few steps with him, hands out, and the steward beside him are drawn
  (reports say stewards and Chelsea staff held him back). He is drawn in his match shirt and shorts, bare-legged, in
  black flip-flops. His confrontation with the referee on the pitch is not shown.
- The cameraman, his camera, the picture through the lens with Drogba's hands on it and the stewards behind him are
  drawn; the narrator says the line, Drogba is not given a voice. Being walked backwards off the pitch by two
  stewards, through the tunnel's doorway and on down the corridor, is drawn. On the way to the camera his hands are
  fists.
- The place of that scene is a set. The camera is put a couple of steps from the tunnel; the tunnel is a plain
  doorway in a blue wall along the front of the stand, the crowd right above it, with a lit service corridor behind
  (the same corridor the referee is walked down in the next picture). It is not a model of Stamford Bridge's tunnel
  or of its East Stand, and the dugouts stand in front of that wall.
- **Invented, not drawn from anything:** the middle finger he gives the camera while the stewards hold him. It was put
  in at the author's request (11 November 2026). No report and no footage shows one: what the reports describe is a
  finger jabbed at the referee, the camera taken in his hands and the shouted line (one agency report speaks of a
  "finger-waving outburst"); UEFA's charge was for insulting the referee and a breach of sportsmanship. The narration
  does not mention the gesture.
- The film ends with the picture switched off like a television set, the channel's usual ending.
- The referee in a corridor between two policemen: drawn. That police escorted him is his own account
  (FourFourTwo); the corridor, and that he is still in his kit, are not documented.
- 2018: the quote is from a newspaper interview (Marca). The room, his clothes and the microphone held up to him are
  drawn.

## What is said carefully

- The film counts appeals, not penalties: that there were four appeals and none was given is a fact; whether each one
  should have been a penalty is an opinion, and the film does not give one. Øvrebø's own words are "there were several
  errors and everyone will have their opinion of those plays" (Marca, 2018).
- Abidal's red card was also disputed (replays show little contact): the film says only that he was sent off.
- The outburst: the full line included a swear word; the film uses "It's a disgrace", as most reports quote it.
- "A three-match ban" is what he served. UEFA's first decision was six matches with two suspended.

## Left out

- Hiddink called it the worst refereeing performance he had seen; John Terry spoke of "six or seven" claims.
- José Bosingwa called the referee a "thief" on television and was banned too; Chelsea were fined €100,000.
- Dani Alves's booking and Abidal's red card kept both out of the final. Barcelona beat Manchester United 2–0 in Rome
  on 27 May and won the treble.
- Øvrebø refereed in the Champions League again the next season, and resigned from the FIFA list in May 2010.
