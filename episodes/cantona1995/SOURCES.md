# "Feet first" (Eric Cantona, Selhurst Park, 1995) — sources

Crystal Palace 1–1 Manchester United, Premier League, Selhurst Park, Wednesday 25 January 1995 (an evening match).
Attendance 18,224, referee Alan Wilkie; 0–0 at half-time, David May 56' (57'), Gareth Southgate 79' (81'). Early in the
second half Eric Cantona kicked out at Palace defender Richard Shaw and was sent off. Walking along the touchline toward
the tunnel, he was abused by a Palace fan who had run down to the front; he jumped over the barrier feet first into the
crowd. Manchester United fined him and kept him out for the rest of the season; the FA banned him to 30 September 1995.
A court gave him two weeks in prison for assault (23 March 1995), cut on appeal to community service; afterwards he gave
his "seagulls" press conference. The coda, added at the author's word: on 11 May 1997 he lifted the Premier League trophy
at Old Trafford as United's captain; a week later the club announced that he was retiring, at 30.

## Left out on purpose, or not known

- **What the fan shouted** is disputed (the abuse reported by witnesses; his own version at his trial, "Off! Off! Off!
  It's an early bath for you"). The film says "shouts abuse" and shows no words (a speech bubble of symbols).
- **The fan is not named** and drawn as an anonymous figure in a dark jacket.
- **The hours of community service** differ (120 in most accounts, 150 in others): the film gives none.
- **"Eleven rows"** comes from one source only: the film says "runs down to the front".
- **The sip of water** at the press conference is in one account only: not narrated.
- **The punches** after the kick and the fan's own conviction are not in the film.
- **The kit man is not named** in the film (a United man in a dark tracksuit walks ahead of him).
- **The day of the retirement** differs by one between the accounts (18 or 17 May 1997): the film says "May 1997", the
  author's words. He was not at the announcement (the chairman made it); the film does not show the announcement.
- **The press conference's date** (after the appeal, 31 March 1995 by the ITV caption) is not shown.
