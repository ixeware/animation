# "The best player in the world sent it over the bar" — sources

Brazil 0–0 Italy after extra time, Brazil win 3–2 on penalties. FIFA World Cup final, the Rose Bowl, Pasadena,
California, Sunday 17 July 1994, 12:30 local time. Attendance 94,194. Referee: Sándor Puhl (Hungary).

The brief (the user's): the key moments, the 0–0 after extra time, the shoot-out, Roberto Baggio's penalty over the bar,
and an ending on the famous image of Baggio standing alone at the spot with his head down. The settings are those of
`plea2005` (STYLE_BIBLE rule 68): 4K, the same look, voice, mix and TV switch-off ending, and an opening (0–10 s) that
can be cut out whole.

Then, at the author's request:
- the first frame shows the real photograph of him with his name over it (viewers did not recognise him from the
  drawing and scrolled on), and the film plays as one take, with no hard cuts, and ends in a slow, sad close;
- his ponytail, and the words "THE MAN WHO DIED STANDING" over the end;
- (with the photograph in `photos/iconic.jpg`) a shorter, natural ponytail; the photograph at the start and at the end,
  "to show how it was in real life"; every shot in motion; the length cut to about a minute (63.8 s).

## What the film says, and where it comes from

The narration, in full: "The best player in the world. One penalty to keep a World Cup final alive. He sent it over the
bar. His name? Roberto Baggio. / July 17, 1994. Pasadena, a hundred degrees. Brazil against Italy, and Baggio plays with
an injured hamstring. / Two hours, no goals. For the first time, a World Cup final goes to penalties. / Baresi: over the
bar. Márcio Santos: saved. Then four goals in a row. Taffarel saves from Massaro. Dunga scores. 3–2, Brazil. / Italy's
last kick: Baggio. He knows Taffarel always dives, so he aims for the middle. Taffarel dives. The ball flies over the bar.
/ Brazil are world champions for the fourth time. And Baggio stays at the spot, alone, head down. Years later he said: if
I could erase a moment from my career, it would be that one."

## Notes

- "A hundred degrees": 100 °F is the figure in the match box on Wikipedia; the plate gives 38 °C as well.
- Baggio's last kick did not decide anything by itself in arithmetic terms: had he scored, Brazil still had a fifth kick
  (Baggio said so himself). The narration says "Italy's last kick", which is true either way; the plate "MISS IT, AND
  BRAZIL ARE CHAMPIONS" is true as written.
- The match clock in the montage jumps: 1' to 75' for Mauro Silva's shot, then to 110' for Romário's miss, then to 120'.
- Cut from the earlier, longer version (still true, no longer in the film): his five goals in the knockout rounds;
  Baresi playing 24 days after knee surgery.
- Pagliuca after the post: one account has him kissing the post, another kissing his glove and touching the post. The
  film shows neither, only the save.
- Right after the match Baggio said: "It was my fault. I'm the one to blame" (UPI). Not in the narration.

## On screen at the end: "THE MAN WHO DIED STANDING"

Added at the author's request, over the last shots. It is a phrase written about Baggio and this moment (for example the
title of a GB News piece, "World Cup tales part 5: Roberto Baggio – The man who died standing"); I did not find who
first said it, and the film does not attribute it to anyone.

## His ponytail

A short tuft tied at the back of the head, hanging to the collar, as in the photograph the author sent. It sits behind
the head, so from the front it is hidden and it comes out beside the neck only as he turns away (seen from the side it
shows plainly, as in the photograph). An earlier, longer tail looked strange (the author) and was drawn on the wrong side
of the head in near-front views; both fixed.

## The iconic picture (`photos/iconic.jpg`)

Sent by the author (STYLE_BIBLE rule 69) on 11 November 2026: first as a 365 × 511 image
(`photos/iconic_small_as_received.png`), then, the same day, a larger copy of the same photograph, 1280 × 1769
(`photos/iconic_as_received.webp`, as received; `iconic.jpg` is made from it, unchanged). The larger copy is cropped about
1% wider on each side; the film uses it. Baggio at the spot after the miss, seen from his right: head down, his right hand on his hip, the
blue number 10 shirt; behind him the advertising boards of the stadium (JVC, Fuji) and the crowd. The boards are the
real ones in the photograph, not drawn. Photographer and agency: not known (the image carries no credit); not credited
on screen.

Used twice:
- 0–2.3 s, under the title ROBERTO BAGGIO / ONE PENALTY / OVER THE BAR, so that viewers know him at once;
- 56.4 s to the end: the drawn Baggio at the spot stops at the place and size he has in the photograph, and the drawing
  dissolves into it ("THE REAL MOMENT"); its colour drains as he speaks, under the quote and his name.

Across the width of a 4K frame the larger copy is enlarged about 1.7 times (the first, small copy needed six and looked
soft).

## What is drawn, and what was not checked

- The kits: Brazil in yellow with blue shorts and white socks, Italy in blue with white shorts and blue socks. The
  goalkeepers' kits (two greys here) were not checked. Shirt numbers from Wikipedia's line-ups (Baggio 10, Baresi 6,
  Massaro 19, Albertini 11, Evani 17, Taffarel 1, Pagliuca 1, Romário 11, Dunga 8, Branco 6, Márcio Santos 15, Mauro
  Silva 5, Cafu 14). The other players are generic figures with real numbers of that squad but not their faces; none of
  the faces is a likeness.
- Which corner each goal went in, which way each goalkeeper dived (except Taffarel to his left for Massaro and for
  Baggio, and Pagliuca to his right for Márcio Santos): not checked; drawn plausibly.
- The shoot-out is drawn at one end; which end it was taken at was not checked.
- The kick-off is not shown (who kicked off was not checked): the two teams wait at the centre spot.
- Which leg his hamstring injury was in was not checked: the right is drawn.
- Baggio's walk from the centre circle is shortened. The order of his walk, the spot and the run-up is as in the footage
  everyone knows, not timed against it.
- The plan drawn over the goal (the arrow for the dive, the ring in the middle at half height) illustrates his own
  account, not a reconstruction of the flight of the ball.
- The banner is redrawn (its words as reported); who held it, and where, was not checked.
- The drawn Baggio alone at the spot (the hook and the end) is drawn after the photograph: right hand on the hip, head
  down, facing the same way. The end stops at 40° from his front, not the photograph's side-on angle, where the flat
  figure breaks up; his place and size match the photograph for the dissolve.
