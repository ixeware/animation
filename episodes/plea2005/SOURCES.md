## What a careful viewer should know (not in the narration)

- **"Stopped a war" is the hook, not a historian's verdict.** The BBC feature itself says Drogba and the team did not
  end the war single-handedly. Most of the fighting had stopped by the end of 2004; the country stayed divided. The
  peace agreement came seventeen months after the plea, out of talks brokered by Burkina Faso. Violence returned after
  the 2010 election, with about 3,000 dead.
- **"Live" is not in the film.** The brief said "live on national TV". The sources I read say a television camera was
  in the room and that the clip was then broadcast again and again; none of them says it went out live. The narration
  says "in front of a national TV camera".
- **Who was in Bouaké.** Voice of America, writing on the day, reports that President Gbagbo did not attend and that
  Guillaume Soro did. Later retellings have the two leaders singing the anthem side by side; the film shows neither.
- Drogba did not score in Omdurman (RSSSF): the film does not say he did.
- Not in the film: the "Flame of Peace" ceremony in the same stadium on 30 July 2007, where weapons were burned.

## What is drawn, and what was not checked

- **The opening image** (a footballer walks between two lines of armed men, who lower their rifles) is a picture of
  the film's first sentence, not an event.
- **Uniforms.** So that the two sides can be told apart at a glance the army is in green with a peaked cap and the
  rebels are in sand with a red beret. That is the film's own convention: in the photograph both wear camouflage (red
  berets and field caps are both there).
- **Kits.** Ivory Coast in white in Omdurman (as in the television frame: white with green) and in orange in Bouaké
  (as in the photograph). Sudan in red, Egypt's goalkeeper in dark grey, Madagascar in red and green: not checked.
  Cameroon in green, red and yellow.
- **Shirt numbers.** Drogba 11. Kalou 8, Yaya Touré 19 and Koné 9 are the numbers they usually wore, not checked for
  that match; the others are arbitrary.
- **The penalty.** That it hit a post is sourced; which post, how high and which way the goalkeeper went were not
  checked.
- **The goals in Bouaké** are five drawn finishes, not staged after footage of the match (none was watched).
- **The joy on the pitch in Omdurman** after the news from Yaoundé is drawn; where the players were when they heard
  was not checked.
- **The dressing room** has the yellow walls of the television frame. In the frame some of the players are standing
  behind those on their knees; in the film the whole squad kneels, as the narration says.
- **The map.** The outline of Ivory Coast and of its neighbours is from the engine's world atlas (Natural Earth,
  1:110m). The line the country was cut along (the "zone of confidence") is drawn approximately: Man, Séguéla, Bouaké
  and Bouna north of it; Duékoué, Daloa, Yamoussoukro and Bondoukou south.
- **The talks.** Two men in suits who shake hands across a table stand for the two delegations; they are not
  likenesses of anyone. The sheet they sign is a prop with an English title.
- **28 March 2007.** He is drawn in a white shirt, holding the trophy up in a stadium; what he wore and where exactly
  he spoke were not checked. The trophy is a generic one.
- **The front page** is a prop, not a real newspaper's page: the date (Monday 4 June 2007), the headline in English as
  it is quoted, the photograph.
- **Timing.** In the film the squad kneels and then he makes the plea; in the quoted words the kneeling comes with
  "we beg you on our knees".
